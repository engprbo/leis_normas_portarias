11 Structural connectivity for connectivity views

The model of the structural connectivity of internal_connectivity_views is found in the connectivity_structure_model schema. Structural connectivity information is described by connectivity_nets, connectivity_busses and connectivity_rippers, which are explained in the following sections. No implementation information is associated with the structural connectivity of an internal_connectivity_view.

11.1 Connectivity nets and sub-nets

A net is defined as a structured representation of a signal. Each net is associated with a signal and it may join single ports. On occasion it is necessary to divide a net into various parts so that properties may be added to one part but not to any of the others. For example, a net takes an input to several sub-blocks, only one path of which is critical for routing purposes. Therefore, it is desirable to assign a high routing priority to that part of the net but not to other parts, so that the circuit will be correctly routed. Hence, a net may be subdivided into sub-nets, which may themselves be further subdivided into sub-nets. However, sub-nets do not give new connectivity information and the ports associated with a sub-net must be referenced in the immediately containing net or sub-net. No two sub-nets at a given level may refer to the same set of ports.

In the information model, the concepts of net and sub-net are generalised into the concept of generic-net. An occurrence of a connectivity_generic_net can be either an occurrence of a connectivity_net or an occurrence of a connectivity_sub_net. Each connectivity_generic_net is associated with a signal. It may join global_ports, instance_member_ports, instance_ports, master_ports and connectivity_rippers (rippers defined in connectivity views). It has attributes such as criticality and delay information. Moreover, it may have connectivity_sub_nets which may be further divided into more connectivity_sub_nets. Figure 11.1: The model of net in connectivity views

A connectivity_net must be defined within the contents of an internal_connectivity_view. Its associated signal and its joined rippers must be defined in the containing view. Furthermore, the joined global_ports, instance_member_ports and master_ports must be mentioned in its associated signal. If a connectivity_net joins an instance_port, then the list of instance_member_ports joined implicitly must be a subset of the instance_member_ports joined by its associated signal.

A connectivity_sub_net must be defined within another connectivity_generic_net. The associated signal of a connectivity_sub_net must be the same as the one in its enclosing structure. Its joined ports or rippers must be a subset of the ports or rippers referenced by its enclosing structure.

11.2 Connectivity busses, bus-slices and sub-busses

A bus is defined as a structured representation of a collection of signals. Each bus is associated with a signal_group and it may join wide ports.

A bus may be subdivided in two orthogonal ways. It may be divided into bus-slices or into sub-busses. A bus-slice is used to define part of a bus. Its associated signal_group is a member of the signal_group associated with its immediately containing structure. No two bus-slices at a given level may refer to the same signal_group. A sub-bus is similar in nature to a sub-net in that it allows additional structural information to be given about a part of the bus. A sub-bus is associated with the same signal_group as that in the immediately containing structure. No two sub-busses at a given level may reference the same set of ports or rippers.

In the information model, the concepts of bus, bus-slice and sub-bus are generalised into the concept of generic-bus. An occurrence of a connectivity_generic_bus can be either an occurrence of a connectivity_bus, an occurrence of a connectivity_bus_slice or an occurrence of a connectivity_sub_bus. Each connectivity_generic_bus is associated with a signal_group. As is the case for connectivity_generic_net, it may join global_ports, instance_member_ports, instance_ports, master_ports and connectivity_rippers. In addition, it may join wide ports such as global_port_bundles, local_master_port_groups, instance_port_bundles and master_port_bundles. It has attributes such as criticality and delay information. Moreover, it may have connectivity_bus_slices or connectivity_sub_busses which may be further divided into more connectivity_bus_slices or connectivity_sub_busses.

All joined global_ports, global_port_bundles, instance_member_ports, local_master_port_groups, master_ports and master_port_bundles must have the same port size. This must be the same as the size of the associated signal_group. Moreover, each single port of the joined port structures must be referenced in the corresponding signal in the flattened signal_group. In the case where a generic bus joins an instance_port or an instance_port_bundle, the pattern of connectivity will be either in the commoned style or in the fanned out style (see section 10). For the commoned style, the joined instance_ports or instance_port_bundles reference master_ports or master_port_bundles whose port size is the same as the size of the flattened signal_group. For the fanned out style, the joined instance_ports or instance_port_bundles are flattened to lists of instance_member_ports. The length of these lists must be equal to the size of the flattened signal_group.

A connectivity_bus must be defined within the contents of an internal_connectivity_view. Its associated signal_group and its joined rippers must be defined in the containing view.

A connectivity_bus_slice must be defined within another connectivity_generic_bus and its associated signal_group must be a member of the signal_group associated with its enclosing structure.

A connectivity_sub_bus must be defined within another connectivity_generic_bus and its associated signal_group must be the same as that of its enclosing structure. Its joined ports or rippers must be a subset of the ports or rippers referenced by its enclosing structure.

11.3 Connectivity rippers

A ripper is used to associate a bus/bus-slice/sub-bus structure with other bus/bus-slice/sub-bus structures or with net/sub-net structures. It replaces the RIPPER cell in EDIF Version 2 0 0. Rippers can be explicitly joined by any number of net structures and bus structures in the same view.

In the information model, the model of the connectivity_ripper has two DERIVEd attributes which identify the associated net structures (i.e. connectivity_generic_net) and bus structures (i.e. connectivity_generic_bus). A constraint is stated which requires that the related bus or net structures belong to the same view. Each structure is related to other structures by sharing at least one common signal.

Part IV: Schematic domain

12 Instantiatable templates for schematic domain

All schematic instantiatable templates share some common attributes, e.g. background colour, documentation. They may have properties which are displayed on the schematic page. The descriptions of the different types of instantiatable template are given below:

  • schematic_offpageconnector_template and schematic_onpageconnector_template

A schematic_offpageconnector_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_offpageconnector on a schematic page. A schematic_onpageconnector_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_onpageconnector on a schematic page. The schematic_offpageconnectors and schematic_onpageconnectors describe aspects of the structural implementation of the logical connectivity for a given level of hierarchy. Both types of template have a hotspot to which connection is to be made graphically. The hotspot has a schematic_wire_style indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure (i.e. schematic_generic_net or schematic_generic_bus). Display slot information may be given to allow the display of the implementation name and the associated interconnect name.

  • schematic_master_port_representation_template and schematic_symbol_port_representation_template

A schematic_master_port_representation_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_master_port_representation or a schematic_master_port_bundle_representation on a schematic page. A schematic_symbol_port_representation_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_symbol_port_representation or a schematic_symbol_port_bundle_representation in an internal_schematic_symbol. Both types of template have a hotspot to which connection is to be made graphically. The hotspot has a schematic_wire_affinity indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure (i.e. schematic_generic_net or schematic_generic_bus). In addition, these templates have two indicators: the schematic_port_representation_style identifies whether the associated master port is narrow or wide, the port_direction_indicator gives its suitability for association with an input, output, bidirectional or unspecified direction master port. Display slot information may be specified for the display of the implementation name, the associated port name and various port attributes, such as acload, delay, dcFaninLoad, dcMaxFanin.

  • schematic_global_port_representation_template

A schematic_global_port_representation_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_global_port_representation on a schematic page. A schematic_global_port_representation_template has a hotspot to which connection is to be made graphically. The hotspot has a schematic_wire_style indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure (i.e. schematic_generic_net or schematic_generic_bus). Display slot information may be given to allow the display of the implementation name and the associated global port name.

  • schematic_ripper_template

A schematic_ripper_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_ripper on a schematic page. A schematic_ripper shows that two or more structures (i.e. schematic_generic_nets or schematic_generic_busses) on a schematic page are linked. A schematic_ripper_template has two or more ripper_hotspots to which connections are to be made graphically. A ripper_hotspot has a schematic_wire_style indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure. Display slot information may be given to allow the display of the implementation name.

  • schematic_junction_template

A schematic_junction_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_net_junction or a schematic_bus_junction on a schematic page. A schematic_net_junction shows that at least two schematic_sub_nets are linked. The related sub_nets belong to the same schematic_generic_net. A schematic_bus_junction shows that at least two schematic_sub_busses are linked. As for schematic_net_junction, the related sub_busses belong to the same schematic_generic_bus. A schematic_junction_template has a hotspot to which connection is to be made graphically. The hotspot has a schematic_wire_affinity indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure (i.e. schematic_generic_net or schematic_generic_bus). Display slot information may be given to allow the display of the implementation name.

  • schematic_interconnect_terminator_template

A schematic_interconnect_terminator_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_interconnect_terminator on a schematic page. A schematic_interconnect_terminator is used to indicate the end of an interconnect. It is sometimes referred to as a dangling end. It has a hotspot to which connection is to be made graphically. The hotspot has a schematic_wire_affinity indicator which gives its suitability for association with a narrow or wide interconnect structure (i.e. schematic_generic_net or schematic_generic_bus). Display slot information may be given to allow the display of the implementation name.

  • page_title_block_template

A page_title_block_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a page_title_block on a schematic page. A page_title_block_template allows details about the design to be placed in a special area, e.g. approved date, page number.

  • page_border_template and schematic_symbol_border_template

A page_border_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a page_border on a schematic page. A schematic_symbol_border_template contains information which is used in the display definition of a schematic_symbol_border for an internal_schematic_symbol.

  • schematic_figure_macro_template

A schematic_figure_macro_template is a collection of figures and annotation text which are themselves associated with figure_groups. It may contain occurrences of other schematic_figure_macro_templates. Occurrences of schematic_figure_macro_templates are used in the same contexts that graphical primitives (i.e. figure) are used; for example, they can be used to implement nets or busses.

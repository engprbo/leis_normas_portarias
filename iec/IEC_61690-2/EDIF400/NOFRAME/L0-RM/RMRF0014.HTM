msbToLsb

msbToLsb ::= '(' 'msbToLsb'
	')'

Used by:

!bitOrder

Similar Keywords:

lsbToMsb

Description:

MsbToLsb specifies that the bit order is from the most-significant bit to the least-significant bit.

Example:

(nameInformation
    (primaryName "a[5:1:-2]"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "a"
            (nameDimension
               (nameDimensionStructure
                  (sequence 5 1 (step 2)))
               (bitOrder (msbTolsb)))))))

Here, the one-dimensional object "a" is specified to be a three-bit object with bit 5 being the most significant and bit 1 being the least significant.

mustJoin

mustJoin ::= '(' 'mustJoin'
	{ interfaceJoined | portRef | weakJoined } 
	')'

Used by:

*interface

Constraints:

1. The following constraint applies to this context: All contexts
A port can be referenced at most once by any interfaceJoined, mustJoin or weakJoined constructs in an interface.
(See also similar constraint in: interfaceJoined, weakJoined)

Description:

MustJoin is used in interface to specify ports of a cell cluster which must be connected externally for correct operation. Only ports defined in the interface of the local cluster (master ports) may be referenced within a mustJoin. InterfaceJoined and weakJoined constructs may be nested with a mustJoin to state more complex relationships between master ports. This relationship forms a tree structure. Within the interface, a port may be referenced only once in interfaceJoined, mustJoin and weakJoined.

The portRef may refer to ports and not to portBundles.

Example:

(mustJoin (portRef A) (portRef B))

The first example states that the master ports A and B must be connected externally for the cell to operate properly. 

(mustJoin
   (interfaceJoined (portRef W1) (portRef W2))
   (interfaceJoined (portRef J1) (portRef J2)))

The second example states that W1 and W2 are joined internally, J1 and J2 are joined internally, and that the pair W1, W2 must be connected externally to the pair J1, J2. Since J1 and J2 are joined internally, either one may be connected to satisfy a connection to the other. This is also true for W1 and W2. Therefore, to satisfy the mustJoin construct, W1 must be connected externally to either J1 or J2, or W2 must be connected externally to J1 or J2.

nameDef

nameDef ::= identifier

Used by:

clusterConfigurationNameDef clusterNameDef connectivityRipperNameDef designHierarchyNameDef designNameDef diagramNameDef documentationNameDef edifNameDef figureGroupNameDef fontNameDef globalPortNameDef hotspotNameDef implementationNameDef instanceNameDef interconnectDelayNameDef interconnectNameDef libraryNameDef libraryObjectNameDef localPortGroupNameDef logicNameDef pageNameDef pcbAssembledPhysicalNetNameDef pcbAuxiliaryComponentNameDef pcbBareBoardCavityNameDef pcbBareBoardCavityWellNameDef pcbBareBoardProbePointNameDef pcbBoardSubsetConstructionFigureNameDef pcbBoardSubsetExtraDetailFigureNameDef pcbBoardSubsetNameDef pcbBoardSubsetPlacementNameDef pcbBoardTerminalNameDef pcbBondPadNameDef pcbComponentAssemblyRuleCriterionNameDef pcbComponentImplementationNameDef pcbComponentNameDef pcbComponentSignalNameDef pcbComponentTerminalGroupNameDef pcbComponentTerminalImplementationNameDef pcbConnectorPartTerminalNameDef pcbDieFiducialNameDef pcbDieStorageConditionsNameDef pcbDimensionedGeometricFigureNameDef pcbDimensionEndGeometryNameDef pcbDimensionNameDef pcbDrawingAnnotationGroupNameDef pcbDrawingAnnotationNameDef pcbDrawingAttributeDefaultNameDef pcbDrawingBorderZonedAreaNameDef pcbDrawingElementNameDef pcbDrawingFigureCollectorNameDef pcbDrawingFillPatternNam

Description:

Every named object must have a defining occurrence as a nameDef. Subsequent references may be made to the object so named, using the same identifier in the role of a nameRef. Named constructs, i.e. constructs which have nameDef (directly or indirectly) as their first required item, provide a scope for any nested name definitions.

A named object is not fully defined, and may not be referenced explicitly, until the closing parenthesis of the definition.

All names in EDIF must be defined before use. The name should be unique for the scope of the defining construct, which is the next higher-named construct, and for its class, which is determined by the type of the nameDef.

There are no reserved names in EDIF. There is no semantic significance to the choice of identifier spelling within a nameDef, except for the structure established through corresponding nameRefs and the display of such names.

Example:

(cell TWOS_COMPLEMENT 
   (cellHeader 
      (nameInformation 
         (primaryName "2s-complement")))
   ...)

This example illustrates a cell defined to be TWOS_COMPLEMENT, a legal EDIF identifier, serving as an alias for the original illegal (in EDIF) name, "2s-complement".

nameDimension

nameDimension ::= '(' 'nameDimension'
	nameDimensionStructure
	{ <bitOrder> } 
	')'

Used by:

*complementedName *complexName

Similar Keywords:

pcbDimensionEndGeometryNameDef pcbDimensionEndGeometryNameRef pcbDimensionNameDef

Description:

NameDimension is used to specify an ordered list of indices of a dimensioned object, such as a schematicBus. The indices specified in nameDimensionStructure can be names of other objects, integers, or an integer range called a sequence. If the indices are associated with bits, then the bitOrder should be specified. A bitOrder of lsbToMsb indicates that the first index is the least-significant bit and that the last index is the most-significant bit. A bitOrder of msbToLsb indicates that the first index is the most-significant bit and that the last index is the least-significant bit. If no bitOrder is specified, then no bit ordering of the indices is implied.

Multiple nameDimensions in the same list indicate that the object has multiple dimensions.

Example:

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "a[b, c, d][3:0]"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "a"
            (nameDimension 
               (nameDimensionStructure "b" "c" "d"))
            (nameDimension
               (nameDimensionStructure
                  (sequence 3 0 (step 1) ))
               (bitOrder (msbToLsb)))))))

In the above example, a two-dimensional object named "a" is specified. The indices of the first dimension are "b", "c" and "d". The indices of the second dimension are 3, 2, 1 and 0, with 3 being the most-significant bit and 0 the least-significant bit. 

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "a[b[3:0], c[3:0], d[3:0]]"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "a"
            (nameDimension
               (nameDimensionStructure
                  (complexName "b"
                     (nameDimension
                        (nameDimensionStructure
                           (sequence 3 0 (step 1) ))
                        (bitOrder (msbToLsb))))
                  (complexName "c"
                     (nameDimension
                        (nameDimensionStructure
                           (sequence 3 0 (step 1) ))
                        (bitOrder (msbToLsb))))
                  (complexName "d"
                     (nameDimension
                        (nameDimensionStructure
                           (sequence 3 0 (step 1) ))
                        (bitOrder (msbToLsb))))))))))

In this example, a one-dimensional object named "a" is specified. It has indices of "b", "c" and "d". Each of these indices is a one-dimensional object name with indices of 3, 2, 1 and 0. In the original system "b", "c" and "d" are related to bits, with 3 being the most-significant bit and 0 the least-significant bit. Note that it is possible that the name in the first example is a different way of specifying the name represented in the second example.

namedUnit

namedUnit ::= '(' 'namedUnit'
	unitNameDef
	namedUnitHeader
	numberOfNewUnits
	numberOfBasicUnits
	unitDimensions
	')'

Used by:

*unitDefinitions

Similar Keywords:

unit

Description:

NamedUnit allows units of measurement to be defined. These are used to define units of distance, angle, etc. in the EDIF data, and they may also be referenced from properties to indicate the unit of measurement for a property.

Most of the units that are used to define new units are SI units. However, other units have been introduced where they are in common usage and derivation in terms of SI units is difficult or inaccurate. Examples of these are fahrenheit and degree.

This construct will supersede unit which has been kept for compatibility with EDIF Version 3 0 0.

Example:

(namedUnit mm 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "mm")))
   1 (e 1 -3) 
   (unitDimensions (meter 1)))

This example shows the definition of millimeter (mm). It indicates that the unit is meter, and that 1 mm is equivalent to 10-3 meters. 

(namedUnit pF 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "pico farad")))
   1 (e 1 -12)
   (unitDimensions (farad 1)))

This example shows the definition of pico farad (pF). It indicates that the unit is farad, and that 1 pF is equivalent to 10-12 farads. 

(namedUnit pound_foot 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "pound foot")))
   1 (e 1383 -4) 
   (unitDimensions (kilogram 1) (meter 1)))

This example shows the definition of pound foot (pound_foot). It indicates that the unit is kg m, and that 1 pound_foot is equivalent to 0.1383 kg m. 

(namedUnit knot 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "knot")))
   1 (e 5148 -4) 
   (unitDimensions (meter 1) (second -1)))

This example shows the definition of knot. It indicates that the unit is m/s, and that 1 knot is equivalent to 0.5148 m/s. 

(namedUnit rpm 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "rpm")))
   60 1 
   (unitDimensions (hertz 1)))
(namedUnit rpm_approx 
   (namedUnitHeader (nameInformation (primaryName "rpm")))
   1 (e 1047 -4) 
   (unitDimensions (radian 1) (second -1)))

This example shows the definition of revolutions per minute (rpm). It indicates that the unit is hertz, and that 60 rpm is equivalent to 1 Hz. The second example shows a less-accurate definition in terms of radians per second.

This example shows that the name I$1 is the name that the originating system had for an object.

The above example shows how a port with the name "1" in the originating system should be represented.

namePartSeparator

namePartSeparator ::= '(' 'namePartSeparator'
	stringToken
	')'

Used by:

*complementedName *complementedNamePart *complexName

Description:

A namePartSeparator is one or more characters which is used in the original system to separate parts of a complex name or complemented name. EDIF associates no semantics with these characters, only that the original system knows that they are part of the original name only as a name part separator. Although the original system may imply additional meaning to the separator, EDIF at this time does not.

Example:

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "clock&enable"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "clock"
            (namePartSeparator "&" )
            "enable" ))))

Here in the originalName clock&enable, the character "&" separates the name parts "clock" and "enable".

nameStructure

nameStructure ::= '(' 'nameStructure'
	( simpleName | complexName | complementedName )
	')'

Used by:

<nameAlias <primaryName

Similar Keywords:

nameDimensionStructure

Description:

Many systems use special characters and sub-strings embedded within an object name to indicate information about the object, e.g. logical complement, boolean relationships, dimensionality, bit ranges, indices, etc. NameStructure is used to specify in detail the structural parts of a name without the use of special characters. NameStructure is also used to apply a certain semantic meaning to each of the name's parts.

NameStructure should be used if the sending system uses the name structure to specify the structure of the design object. For example, a schematicBus might have a name in the original system "DATA(a, b, c)", which implies that the bus "DATA" contains the signals "a","b" and "c". The characters '(', ')' and ',' are used to specify structure. This name should be specified in EDIF as a complexName.

Suppose a signal name "a" in the original system contains no structural information. This should be specified in EDIF as an originalName with no nameStructure. A name such as "~a", where the character '~' implies complement in the original system, should be specified in EDIF as a complementedName within nameStructure.

Where EDIF has provided syntax for representing the semantics implied by special characters, the special characters should be represented by the appropriate EDIF syntax.

When reading EDIF, a translator should re-assemble the name in the receiving system using the naming rules of that system. For example, a bus name "~DATA[0:3]" might be translated to "NDATA(0-3)".

There are no rules in EDIF about the uniqueness of a nameStructure specification, because the scope of nameStructure is dependent on the originating system.

Examples:

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "B1"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "B"
            (nameDimension 
               (nameDimensionStructure 1))))))

In this first example, the receiving system may use the simpleName B and the index value 1 to generate a name according to its own conventions, e.g. "B(1)" or "B1". 

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "~(a.~b)[m, d[0:3], n]"
      (nameStructure
         (complementedName 
            "a"
            (namePartSeparator "." )
            (complementedNamePart "b" )
            (nameDimension 
               (nameDimensionStructure 
                  "m"
                  (complexName "d"
                     (nameDimension
                        (nameDimensionStructure
                           (sequence 0 3))))
                  "n"))))))

In this second example, the receiving system might map this name to ".NOT.(a.AND..NOT.b)[m, d0, d1, d2, d3, n]", assuming that the translator maps the namePartSeparator "." to ".AND.". 

(nameInformation
   (primaryName "a[1, 5, 7][0:8:2]"
      (nameStructure
         (complexName "a"
            (nameDimension 
               (nameDimensionStructure 1 5 7))
            (nameDimension 
               (nameDimensionStructure
                  (sequence 0 8 (step 2))))))))

In this third example, the specification indicates that the object is two dimensional.

nonPermutable

nonPermutable ::= '(' 'nonPermutable'
	{ permutable | portRef } 
	')'

*permutable

1. The following constraint applies to this context: All contexts
The number of portRefs used inside permutable or nonPermutable constructs should be equal when they are nested in further permutable or nonPermutable constructs.
(See also similar constraint in: permutable)

2. The following constraint applies to this context: All contexts
A port can be referenced at most once by any permutable or nonPermutable constructs in an interface.
(See also similar constraint in: permutable)

Description:

NonPermutable is used to describe ports or groups of ports which cannot be interchanged. Used in conjunction, the nonPermutable and permutable constructs define a permutability tree structure of ports within the interface. The order of portRefs is significant, as in the schematic below.

In the context of a permutable construct, corresponding ports may be exchanged as a whole or not at all; partial exchanges are not permitted.

nonPermutable schematic

Example:

(permutable
   (nonPermutable (portRef A) (portRef B))
   (nonPermutable (portRef C) (portRef D)))

The example states that the ports A and B are not permutable, and also that the ports C and D are not permutable. However, the ports A and C may be permuted as long as the ports B and D are permuted at the same time. This structure could be appropriate for the previously-illustrated schematic.

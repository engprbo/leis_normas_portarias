26 Drawings

The scope of the drawing model is to describe the drawings used to explain or aid the manufacture, repair or testing of a PCB/MCM. These drawings include:

  • Drill drawings showing the positions and sizes of drilled holes

  • Assembly drawings showing the positions of components assembled onto the bare board

  • Dimensioned drawings which document the position and size of the assembled board

26.1 Drawing borders

A pcb_drawing may contain a drawing_border which describes the border of a drawing. It is defined by instantiating a drawing_border_template. Figure 26.1 shows an example of a drawing_border.

Figure 26.1: An example of a drawing_border

26.2 Drawing title blocks

A pcb_drawing may contain a drawing_title_block which holds the title block information of a drawing. It is defined by instantiating a drawing_title_block_template. A title block is an area on a drawing sheet which contains the information required for the identification of the drawing. It may include information such as company name, sheet size, date of drawing. Figure 26.2 shows an example of a drawing_title_block which is placed in the lower right-hand corner of the drawing.

26.3 Annotation

Annotation is the placement of textual notes, with or without associated leaders. An annotation_note describes an annotation that may contain text, graphics and symbols. An annotation_group provides a grouping of annotation_notes that are semantically related. Purpose of the grouping is stated in the annotation_group_purpose. Figure 26.3 shows examples of annotation_group.

Figure 26.3: Examples of annotation_group

26.4 Board subset placements

A board_subset_definition specifies a subset of the PCB/MCM which is to be placed on a drawing. Its main purpose is to allow the visualisation of chosen aspects of the board, e.g. tracks or component outlines. The subsetting is done by the inclusion of the geometries collected in figure collectors (see fabricate_figure_collector_inclusion and drawing_figure_collector_inclusion). When a board_subset_definition is used to describe a drill drawing, it specifies a collection of drill_symbol_selections. Each selection chooses a drill_symbol to represent a drill_rule used on the board. Similarly, when a board_subset_definition is used to describe an assembly drawing, it specifies a collection of component_symbol_selections. Each selection chooses a component_symbol to represent all those packaged or die components on the board that are mounted by a particular mountable_package or mountable_die_physical_template. In addition, a board_subset_definition may specify a clipping outline (via board_subset) in order to display a certain region of the board.

A board_subset_definition is placed on a drawing by specifying a board_subset_placement which allows the board subset to be scaled, rotated and positioned anywhere on the drawing.

26.5 Dimensioning

This section describes the concept of dimensioning used on pcb drawings. The model of dimension can be found in the pcb_drawing_model schema. A dimension is used to indicate measurements of distances or angles on a pcb_drawing. The model does not describe the detailed annotation required for dimensions; instead it models specifications which a target system could use to generate the annotation for dimensions. Some elements of dimensioning are shown in figure 26.4.

Figure 26.4: Some elements of dimension

A dimension is divided into several categories. Figure 26.5 shows the model of dimension.

Figure 26.5: The model of dimension

26.5.1 Linear dimensioning

A linear_dimension is used to specify distance dimensions between drawing features. The simplest case of linear_dimension is that of a single_linear_dimension which is used to measure the distance between two points. This is illustrated in figure 26.4(a). However, single_linear_dimension can also be used to measure a radius or diameter. Its purpose is specified by the linear_type flag. The dimensioned object is of circular cross section (where the plane of cross section is orthogonal to the plane of the drawing) and either a radius or diameter is to be dimensioned. This requires the generated dimension value to be qualified by an appropriate radius symbol or diameter symbol prefix (or suffix) (e.g R30 for a radius of 30 units or Ø60 for a diameter of 60 units).

A linear_dimension can also be a multiple_linear_dimension which groups related sets of linear dimensions. There are two types of multiple_linear_dimension, namely, datum_linear_dimension and chained_linear_dimension.

Datum linear dimension

A datum_linear_dimension is a grouping of dimensioning information for features of the drawing which are to be dimensioned from a common datum origin (ISO 129-1985 (E) calls this form of dimensioning `parallel dimensioning'). An example of this type of dimensioning is shown in figure 26.6(a) below and in figure 26.6(b) a special variation called, in ISO 129, a "superimposed running dimensioning" is illustrated. For parallel dimensioning, the offset of each dimension line is different. On the other hand, for superimposed running dimensioning, all dimension line offsets are the same.

Figure 26.6: Examples of datum_linear_dimension
Chained linear dimension

A chained_linear_dimension is another form of multiple_linear_dimension. In this form of dimensioning, dimension spans are linked in a chain so that the end limit of one span is the start limit for the next span. Frequently all dimension line offsets for a chained_linear_dimension are equal and the dimension lines of the spans are thus co-linear; this is illustrated in figure 26.7 (a). It is, however, possible for chained_linear_dimension to have differing dimension line offsets for the spans; this is illustrated in figure 26.7 (b).

Figure 26.7: Examples of chained_linear_dimension
Horizontal dimensioning, vertical dimensioning & direct dimensioning

Most of the examples illustrated so far show the dimension lines drawn horizontally. This form of dimensioning is called horizontal_dimension. To draw vertical dimension lines, a vertical_dimension is used. Finally, another variant of dimensioning is direct_dimension in which dimension lines are drawn parallel to the line connecting the dimension points. The three categories are illustrated in figure 26.8.

Figure 26.8: Examples of horizontal, vertical and direct dimensioning

For horizontal_dimension, the dimension value of each dimension span corresponds to the horizontal distance between dimension points. Dimension lines are drawn parallel to the x-axis of the drawing. All projection lines are required to project vertically (parallel with the drawing y-axis) from the dimension points.

For vertical_dimension, the dimension value of each dimension span corresponds to the vertical distance between dimension points. Dimension lines are drawn vertically and thus parallel to the y-axis of the drawing. All projection lines are required to project horizontally (parallel with the drawing x-axis) from the dimension points;

For direct_dimension, the distance of each span is measured directly between corresponding dimension points. Dimension lines are parallel to the line joining the dimension points. All projection lines are either perpendicular to the line joining the dimension points, or if a projection_line_angle is specified, are projected at the specified angle to the positive-going x-axis of the drawing. Examples of this form of dimensioning are given in figure 26.9 below. Figure 26.10 shows the model of linear_dimension.

26.5.2 Circular dimensioning

The circular_dimension is categorised into radial_dimension and diametric_dimension. A radial_dimension is used to specify the dimensioning for the radius of an arc or a circle. A diametric_dimension is used to specify the dimensioning for the diameter of a circle. The circular_dimensions are illustrated in figure 26.11. Figure 26.12 shows the model of circular_dimension.

26.5.3 Angular dimensioning

The angular_dimension has some characteristics similar to linear_dimension. It is either a single_angular_dimension or a multiple_angular_dimension. An example of single_angular_dimension is illustrated in figure 26.4(b) and examples of multiple_angular_dimension are illustrated in figure 26.13. The limits for the dimensioning of angles are specified by the dimensioned line segments. A pair of line segments is used to define each angle. The projected intersection of the identified line segments should be used as the centre for generating each dimension arc at a radius specified by the dimension_arc_offset. Projection lines should extend (with a gap specified by the projection_line_gap) from the ends of the line segments further from the point of intersection to a distance beyond the dimension arc specified by the projection_line_overshoot. 26.5.4 Positioning of dimensioning

There are dimensioning items which define reference points and lines relative to which dimensioning elements should be positioned. Some of the information required for the positioning of dimensioning is illustrated in figure 26.15.

Linear dimensioning start and end points

A linear_dimension requires a start point and an end point which are used as limit markers for the extents of dimensioning and points of origin for offsets. The start point is specified in linear_dimension. For a single_linear_dimension, the end point is specified in a single linear_dimension_item. For a multiple_linear_dimension, multiple spans of dimensioning are required and therefore multiple end points are needed to mark the end of each span. The details for each span, including the end point, are held in a corresponding linear_dimension_item. This is illustrated in figure 26.16.

Figure 26.16: Linear dimensioning start and end points
Angular dimensioning start and end lines

For angular dimensioning, the start line segment and the end line segment and the defining start point and end point for each segment are used to define the angle to be measured and as reference features for the positioning of dimensioning elements. In figure 26.17, the way in which these features are used to define the angle to be dimensioned is explained. In many cases, dimension arcs are drawn with arrows at both ends and so it is the magnitude and not the sign of the angle which is dimensioned. Circular dimension start and end points

The positioning of the dimension line and associated dimensioning elements follows rules similar to those for linear_dimension. The derivation of the start and end points is illustrated in figure 26.19. For radial_dimension, the start point is the centre of the dimensioned circle or arc and the end point is the point of intersection between the circle or arc and a line projected from the start point in the direction given by dimension_line_angle. For diametric_dimension, the end point is the intersection of the dimensioned circle and a line projected through the circle centre in the direction of the dimension_line_angle and the start point is the intersection produced by projecting the line in the opposite direction.

Figure 26.19: Circular dimensioning derived start and end points
dimension_line_offset & dimension_arc_offset

The dimension_line_offset controls the distance by which dimension lines are offset from the features within the dimensioned objects. It is specified in linear_dimension_item and circular_dimension. Similarly, the dimension_arc_offset controls the distance by which dimension arcs are offset from the features within the dimensioned objects. It is specified in angular_dimension_item.

For linear_dimension_item, the start_point attribute identifies the sole point of reference for dimension_line_offset. When multiple_linear_dimension is used, the dimension_line_offset of each span of dimensioning (specified in the corresponding linear_dimension_item) is measured relative to the common start_point.

For a circular_dimension, the method of deriving a start point and an end point is described in figure 26.19. The effect of the dimension_line_offset on a circular_dimension is illustrated in figure 26.20.

Figure 26.20: Effect of dimension_line_offset on circular_dimension

For an angular_dimension, the start_line identifies a line segment which acts as the base for the angle(s) to be measured. The end point of the start_line is the sole point of reference for the attribute dimension_arc_offset. This includes the multiple_angular_dimension where the dimension_arc_offset of each span of dimensioning (specified in the corresponding angular_dimension_item) is relative to this common reference point.

dimension line and arc extension

Sometimes it is not convenient, because of space limitations, to place dimension text close to its associated dimension line or arc. For such cases provision has been made for the nearest end of the dimension line or arc to be extended with a path of leader lines linking the dimension line or arc with the dimension text. The choice of extension line path styles is limited to those enumerated by dimension_line_extension_type. Figure 26.21 shows the effect of dimension_line_extension_type on a horizontal_dimension.

For no_extension, there is no leader extension line. For single_line_extension, the dimension line is extended to either be level with the extreme end of the text, or if the line passes through the text, it is drawn to stop just short of the text. For both double_line_extension_to_text_centre and double_line_extension_to_underline_text, the dimension line is extended on the side closest to the dimension text and then the path is continued by a vertical line (in the case of horizontal_dimension) which ends in close proximity to the dimension text. In the case of double_line_extension_to_text_centre, the path ends just short of the start of the text and is aligned centrally with respect to character height. In the case of double_line_extension_to_underline_text, provided the text is oriented parallel to the extension end, the path extends to underline the text; otherwise, it extends to come close to the far side of the text and finishes to align with bottom of the text.

dimension_text_offset

The dimension_text_offset specifies the displacement of the bottom of the dimension text relative to the centre of the dimension line or arc. This occurs as an attribute of linear_dimension_item, angular_dimension_item and circular_dimension.

projection_line_gap

The projection_line_gap is the gap left between each dimensioned object and the corresponding dimensioning projection lines. This attribute acts globally on all dimensioning and is defined at the level of the pcb_drawing.

projection_line_overshoot

The projection_line_overshoot is the extent to which each projection line extends beyond any corresponding dimension line or arc. This attribute acts globally on all dimensioning and is defined at the level of the pcb_drawing.

Tolerancing of dimensions

If toleranced dimensions are to be generated, tolerance_data specifies the positive tolerance value and the negative tolerance value which apply to the measured value. The tolerance_type determines how the tolerance text is generated. Figure 26.25 shows the effect of tolerance_type on tolerance text. For nominal_with_deltas tolerancing, the tolerance text is potentially a different height to the main text; whereas in minimum_and_maximum tolerancing, the two text strings are drawn at the same height as each other (and equal to the height of the main dimension text).

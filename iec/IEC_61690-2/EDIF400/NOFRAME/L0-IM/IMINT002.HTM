4 Connectivity

This chapter describes the concept of connectivity in EDIF. The view types that support connectivity definitions are connectivity, schematic, PCB/MCM, masklayout and symboliclayout. The concepts of logical connectivity and structural connectivity are explicitly introduced.

4.1 Logical connectivity

The logical connectivity information describes the connectivity for a given level of hierarchy. It is defined in terms of instances (see section 2.6), signals and signal_groups. The information carried in the logical connectivity section is similar in each of the connectivity-carrying types of view. The signals and signal_groups are used to describe the overall connectivity for the entire view. The model for the logical connectivity section can be found in the logical_connectivity_model schema.

4.1.1 Signals

A signal is defined as an object which lists all the global_ports (defined in edif), instance_member_ports (ports on members of instances) and master_ports (ports defined in the cluster_interface) which are electrically common at a given level of hierarchy. A signal is always one-bit wide and is defined within a view, i.e. internal_connectivity_view, internal_schematic_view and internal_pcb_mcm_view. All global_ports joined by a signal are defined in the containing edif. All instance_member_ports joined by a signal must refer to instances in the containing view. Finally, all master_ports joined by a signal must be defined in the interface of the containing cluster. Figure 4.1 shows the model of signal.

4.1.2 Signal groups

A signal_group provides a grouping mechanism for signals. A signal_group does not establish connectivity. It is defined within a view and is used to support structural connectivity. A signal_group is specified in terms of an ordered list of one or more signals or signal_groups in the same containing view. The structure of a signal_group is independent of port ordering or port structure. No two signal_groups may have the same structure of members. The same signal may be referenced in the ordered signal list of more than one signal_group. The same signal may be referenced more than once in a single signal_group.

Figure 4.2 shows an example of signal_groups and signals. Boxes with thick borders represent signal_groups and those with narrow borders represent signals. The figure shows how signals can be built up into signal_groups, and these in turn included in further signal_groups. This hierarchical structure can then be used to support the structural connectivity expressed by busses.

4.2 Structural connectivity

The structural connectivity describes the structural implementation of the logical connectivity for a given level of hierarchy. It is defined in terms of nets, busses, rippers and junctions. The structural connectivity section is viewtype specific. Table 4.1 shows which structural connectivity items are allowed in different view types. In connectivity or schematic views, wide instances are available. Connection to a wide instance is discussed in chapter 10. The structural connectivity of connectivity views is described in chapter 11 and the structural connectivity of schematic views is described in chapter 13.

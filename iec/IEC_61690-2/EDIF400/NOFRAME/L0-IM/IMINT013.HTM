18 Bare dies and bare die attachment

An MCM normally contains bare die components. A bare die component is an unpackaged integrated circuit which is mounted directly onto or into the bare board.

18.1 Die bonding technologies

Four common techniques which are used to bond bare dies to the substrate are described below. Combinations of these technologies can be used on an MCM.

Wirebonding

In wirebonding connection technology, bare dies are attached to the substrate with many of the bond pads facing up. Fine wires, usually made of gold, are attached to the die bond pads and metallised pads on the substrate, thus forming a connection. Figure 18.1 shows a wirebonded die on the substrate.

Figure 18.1: Wirebonding
Tape Automated Bonding (TAB)

In TAB connection technology, bare dies are bonded to a carrier which is part of a long roll of tape with metallised pads and wiring patterns deposited on it or within it. This process is called "inner lead bonding". Then, individual carriers can be cut out of the tape and pads on the carrier can be bonded to pads on the substrate by "outer lead bonding". Figure 18.2 shows a TAB die on the substrate.

Figure 18.2: Tape automated bonding
Flip-chip

In flip-chip connection technology, solder bumps are formed on the bond pads of bare dies. Then, bare dies are placed face down on the substrate so that the bumps align with metallized pads on the substrate. By reflowing the solder, all the contacts are made simultaneously. Figure 18.3 shows a solder bumped die on the substrate.

Figure 18.3: Flip-chip bonding
Embedded die (or chips-first interconnect)

In embedded die connection technology, bare dies are placed in cavities on the substrate and then the interconnect structure is fabricated in layers over the embedded dies. Connections are made to the bond pads by vias within the overlying layers. Figure 18.4 shows an embedded die on the substrate.

Figure 18.4: Embedded die

18.2 Description of dies

The model of die description is defined in the mcm_die_physical_model schema. A die_physical_template, as shown in figure 18.5, defines a collection of bond_pads which specify the physical locations on a die where contact may be made. A die description is either internal (i.e. internal_die_physical_template) or external (i.e. external_die_physical_template) to an EDIF file. An internal_die_physical_template specifies a complete description of a die, containing information such as body_volume and occupied_space. It may also specify a collection of fiducials (i.e. die_fiducial) which serve as reference points for alignment purposes. If a die contains one or more bond_pads which are pre-tabbed (as shown in figure 18.2), it should also specify the tabbing material.

Figure 18.5: The model of die_physical_template

18.3 Die bond pads

A bond_pad defines a connection place for a die and it is either electrical (i.e. electrical_bond_pad) or non-electrical (i.e. non_electrical_bond_pad). A bond_pad specifies the die_side (either top or bottom) that it resides on. A bond_pad description is either internal (i.e. internal_bond_pad) or external (i.e. external_bond_pad) to an EDIF file. An internal_bond_pad specifies a complete description of a bond pad. It specifies the bond_pad_template which is instantiated and the position of the template on the die (as indicated by the fixed_scale_transform information). Furthermore, an internal_bond_pad is bare, pre-tabbed or pre-bumped. If it is a pre-tabbed pad (i.e. internal_pre_tabbed_bond_pad) as shown in figure 18.2, it specifies the tabbed_bond_pad_template which is instantiated and also the template position. In addition, it specifies the TAB lead shape and the TAB lead material. On the other hand, if it is a pre-bumped pad (i.e. internal_pre_bumped_bond_pad) as shown in figure 18.3, it specifies the solder_bump_template which is instantiated and also the bump origin. Figure 18.6 shows the model of bond_pad.

Figure 18.6: The model of bond_pad

18.4 Mounting of dies

The model of die mounting is defined in the mcm_mountable_die_physical_model schema. A mountable_die_physical_template describes the result of mounting a die_physical_template (see section 18.2) onto an MCM in a particular way. It may define a collection of mountable_bond_pads, each of which defines how a bond_pad of a die_physical_template should be bonded to the MCM. A mountable die description is either internal (i.e. internal_mountable_die_physical_template) or external (i.e. external_mountable_die_physical_template) to an EDIF file. An internal_mountable_die_physical_template specifies a complete description of a mountable die. It specifies the mounted die_physical_template and the side of the MCM (i.e. mounting_side) it is mounted onto. It also records the additional auxiliary_components that should be added to achieve this mounting. If a die is tabbed during mounting, it indicates the tabbing material surrounding the mounted die. As shown in figure 18.3, the flip-chip technology places the mounted die face down onto the MCM. Hence, the mountable die description has a boolean flag indicating whether or not a die is flipped during mounting. Finally, it may also specify a die_position_tolerance which indicates the die placement tolerance (i.e. the expected maximum variation in placement position). Figure 18.7 shows the model of mountable_die_physical_template.

Figure 18.7: The model of mountable_die_physical_template

18.5 Mounting of die bond pads

A mountable_bond_pad describes how to attach a die bond pad onto an MCM. Hence, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the bond_pads and the mountable_bond_pads. That is, each bond_pad of a die_physical_template has a matching mountable_bond_pad in all the possible mountable_die_physical_templates. Figure 18.8 shows the model of mountable_bond_pad.

Figure 18.8: The model of mountable_bond_pad

Just as a mountable die description is either internal or external to an EDIF file, a similar concept applies to its mountable_bond_pad. An internal_mountable_bond_pad specifies a complete description of a mountable bond pad. It chooses the bond_pad which is to be mounted and also selects the material to be used for attachment. As shown in figure 18.6, a bond_pad can be bare, pre-tabbed or pre-bumped. When mounting a die, it is not possible to tab or bump an already pre-tabbed or pre-bumped bond_pad. On the other hand, a bare bond_pad can be tabbed, bumped, wirebonded, or left bare during the die mounting process. This distinction is reflected in the subtype hierarchy of internal_mountable_bond_pad as shown in figure 18.8.

An internal_mountable_bare_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a bare bond_pad. In this case, the bond_pad is left as bare for that particular mounting method. An internal_mountable_pre_tabbed_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a pre-tabbed bond_pad. An internal_mountable_pre_bumped_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a pre-bumped bond_pad. In both cases, no further mounting information is provided as it is not possible to tab or bump an already pre-tabbed or pre-bumped bond_pad on a die. An internal_mountable_tabbed_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a bare bond_pad using tabbing. As with the internal_pre_tabbed_bond_pad, it specifies the tabbed_bond_pad_template which is instantiated and also the template position. In addition, it specifies the TAB lead shape and the TAB lead material. An internal_mountable_bumped_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a bare bond_pad using bumping. Similar to the internal_pre_bumped_bond_pad, it specifies the solder_bump_template which is instantiated and also the bump origin. Finally, an internal_mountable_wirebonded_bond_pad describes the result of mounting a bare bond_pad using wirebonding. It specifies the wirebond_template which is instantiated and also the wirebond origin.
